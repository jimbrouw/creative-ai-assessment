## Narrative Arc

### Setup (Context)
The Hyatt Regency Kansas City was a architectural showpiece. Two suspended walkways crossed the atrium, stacked one above the other. On July 17, 1981, the hotel hosted a tea dance with 1,600 people.

### Conflict (The Mistake)
During construction, a seemingly minor change was made to how the walkways were suspended. The original design called for one continuous rod through both walkways. The contractor proposed using two shorter rods instead. The change was approved over the phone in 30 seconds. No one recalculated the load.

### Escalation
- The new design doubled the load on the upper connection
- The walkways had been in use for a year
- That night, 1,600 people were dancing below

### Climax (The Collapse)
At 7:05 PM, the fourth-floor walkway connection failed. Both walkways collapsed onto the crowd below. 114 people died. 216 were injured.

### Resolution (The Aftermath)
Investigation revealed the phone-approved change was the cause. The engineers lost their licenses—the first time professional engineers were held accountable for structural failure. Building codes changed nationwide.

### Meaning (Why It Matters)
This wasn't unique. It was a systems failure that exists everywhere complex projects are built under pressure. The lesson isn't "engineers are careless"—it's "small changes in complex systems need verification."
