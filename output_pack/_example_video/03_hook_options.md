# Hook Options: The Engineering Mistake That Killed 2,000 People

> This is a template file showing the expected format.

---

## Option A: Cold Open (Recommended)

**Hook text:**
> In 1981, a Kansas City hotel became the site of America's deadliest structural collapse. The cause? A change that took 30 seconds to approve. No one checked the math.

**Approach**:
- Lead with the dramatic outcome
- Tease the cause (30-second approval)
- End with human error implication

**Word count**: 38 words (~15 seconds)

**Why it works**:
- Immediate stakes (deadliest collapse)
- Specific detail creates curiosity (30 seconds)
- Implies story will explain the "how"

**Risk**:
- Slightly over 5-second target
- May need tightening for strict constraint

---

## Option B: Question Hook

**Hook text:**
> What if a 30-second phone call could kill 114 people? In 1981, it did.

**Approach**:
- Open with provocative question
- Immediately answer it
- Creates instant intrigue

**Word count**: 18 words (~7 seconds)

**Why it works**:
- Very short, punchy
- Question forces engagement
- Specific numbers add credibility

**Risk**:
- "What if" openings can feel overused
- Less context than Option A

---

## Option C: Stakes Hook

**Hook text:**
> On July 17, 1981, 1,600 people gathered in a Kansas City hotel atrium. Within seconds, 114 of them would be dead—because of a change no one verified.

**Approach**:
- Set the scene first
- Contrast normalcy with tragedy
- Blame the unverified change

**Word count**: 32 words (~13 seconds)

**Why it works**:
- Creates vivid mental picture
- Before/after contrast is powerful
- "No one verified" plants the question

**Risk**:
- Takes longer to get to the hook
- "1,600 people gathered" is slightly generic

## Recommendation

**Use Option A (Cold Open)** with minor tightening:

**Optimized version:**
> In 1981, a Kansas City hotel became America's deadliest structural collapse. The cause? A 30-second phone call. No one checked the math.

**Word count**: 27 words (~11 seconds)

**Rationale**:
1. Best balance of clarity and curiosity
2. "30-second phone call" is more vivid than "change"
3. "No one checked the math" is strong closer
4. Sets up entire video narrative
